Introduction

The sporting audience, from screen to stadium.

Australian sport has two audiences: people watching on screens and people entering stadiums. Start by comparing AFL and NRL television reach, then examine the wider streaming environment, geographic restrictions on live AFL broadcasting and the places where crowds gather. These different measures are labelled separately: an estimated TV viewer, an adult using a video service and a match attendance are not interchangeable.

03 / VIEWING HABITS

The changing television landscape.

Australian video habits reveal the broader market in which sports broadcasters operate.

Important distinction: The ACMA charts below describe all video viewing by Australian adults, not sport-specific television audiences. Different media categories can overlap: one person can use several services.

Figure 6 / Streaming, broadcast and short-form video · Advanced slope chart

Broadcast TV recovered in 2025; paid streaming held steady

Choose a medium to focus its change between the ACMA 2024 and 2025 adult surveys. The y-axis shows the share of Australians who used each type of video service during the previous seven days, not time spent watching.

Key finding: Traditional free-to-air television rose from 46% to 52% (+6 percentage points), while paid streaming remained at 68% and short-form video fell from 59% to 52% (−7 points).

Source: ACMA, How we watch and listen to content (March 2026), executive summary. Base: Australian adults, past seven days. Media types overlap; percentage-point changes are not audience counts.

More than two-thirds used paid video streaming

Select one media category to display its 2025 reach as a 100-cell grid. Each cell equals one percentage point of adults, not an individual respondent. The pale squares represent adults outside the selected category.

04 / ATTENDANCE

What changes when fans go to games?

From the national concentration of stadium visits to annual changes in crowd sizes and venue rankings.

Figure 8 / Crowd concentration · Advanced hexagonal bin map

Nearby venues combine into nine attendance clusters

Instead of drawing one circle per stadium, this map groups all 18 venues into equal-sized, approximately 175-km-radius hexagonal bins. Colour represents combined 2025 attendance for venues in the same bin; hover a hexagon to see its venues and total entries. This is a distinct bin map, not another proportional symbol map.

Key finding: A Melbourne-area hexagon containing the MCG, Marvel Stadium and another nearby venue has approximately 4.76 million entries. This aggregation removes marker overlap and reveals the combined concentration, but deliberately hides differences within each hexagon.

Source: AFL Tables, 2025 attendances by venue. Calculated from the same 18 stadium records as Figure 5; all bins together total 8,257,027 crowd entries. Equal-area binning in Australian Albers projection; converted back to geographic coordinates for display. Bin boundaries are analytical, not administrative.

Counting rule: Each attendance is one entry to a match. A person attending twice is counted twice. We use AFL Tables for all the venue and season figures so totals are internally consistent.

Figure 9 / Match-day intensity · Advanced season heatmap

Finals typically draw larger crowds per match

Each cell shows average crowd per game, not season total. Comparing per-match averages makes the 9–11 finals games more comparable with the much larger home-and-away schedule. The 2020–21 pandemic affected attendance.

Key finding: In 2026, finals averaged approximately 68,751 spectators per game versus 37,551 for home-and-away games. COVID-related disruption in 2020–21 is visible in both phases.

Source: AFL Tables, attendances by season (2015–2026). Figures are recorded match entries divided by the number of games with attendance data; finals and home-and-away values are separate.

Figure 10 / Explaining a year-on-year change · Advanced waterfall

How did 2025 finish 29,744 attendances below 2024?

Waterfall bars show the change in crowd entries from 2024 to 2025, not the total number of spectators. Home-and-away crowds declined while finals crowds partly offset that fall.

Key finding: Home-and-away attendances decreased by 97,161 while finals increased by 67,417, giving a net decrease of 29,744 entries from 8,286,771 in 2024 to 8,257,027 in 2025.

Source: AFL Tables, season totals for 2024 and 2025. All figures are event entries rather than unique people. The net difference uses identical scope and source for both seasons.

Figure 11 / A complete attendance whole · Advanced treemap

The MCG accounted for nearly 38% of 2025 AFL crowds

The treemap represents all 18 AFL venues in AFL Tables, so the areas form a complete whole. Each tile’s area is proportional to its total 2025 attendance; small venues remain represented even when they are too narrow to label.

Key finding: The MCG accounted for 3,120,416 of 8,257,027 crowd entries (37.8%). All remaining venues together form the other 62.2%, avoiding the misleading incomplete-whole problem identified in DV1.

Source: AFL Tables, attendances by venue for 2025. The same 18 venues and total are used in Figures 7, 8, 11 and 12. Hover a tile for venue and exact share.
