How Australia Watches Sport.

Exploring who watches Australia's major sporting codes, how broadcasts reach fans, and what happens when supporters head to the stadium.

The sporting audience, from screen to stadium.

Start with who watches sport. See why broadcasting access varies by state; then explore changing viewing habits. Finally, follow crowds from individual stadiums to geographic clusters and across seasons. Each chart answers a new question.

01 / AUDIENCES

What are Australians watching?

Begin with AFL and NRL: compare the scale and composition of their television audiences.

Figure 1 / Television audience reach · AFL vs NRL

AFL reaches more television viewers than NRL

Estimated Australian TV audiences, aged 14+, from July 2025 to June 2026. Hover for the estimates; the colours remain consistent across the audience charts.

Key finding: AFL's estimated TV audience was around 1.7 million people larger than NRL's. These are people who watched at least occasionally, not average viewers of a single match.

Source: Roy Morgan Single Source, July 2025–June 2026 (n=59,959; Australians aged 14+). AFL report (15 Sep 2026) · NRL report (18 Sep 2026). NRL viewers are rounded to one decimal million in the source.

Figure 2 / Audience growth by age · Advanced heatmap

Older age groups led TV audience growth in 2024

Percentage growth in Australians aged 14+ who watched AFL or NRL television coverage in 2024 compared with 2023, grouped by age. Darker cells indicate faster growth. Hover to compare both years’ estimated viewer counts.

Key finding: The 65+ audience grew fastest for both codes (AFL +6.5%; NRL +6.6%). The slowest growth was among AFL viewers aged 50–64 (+1.0%) and NRL viewers under 35 (+1.9%).

Source: Roy Morgan, AFL vs NRL television audiences, 2023–2024 (published 18 March 2025), age chart on page 2 of the original report. Surveys: Jan–Dec 2023 (n=64,994) and Jan–Dec 2024 (n=64,823); Australians aged 14+. Growth rates are those reported by Roy Morgan. This is a different period from Figure 1 (July 2025–June 2026).

Figure 3 / Gender and television audience reach · Advanced dumbbell

AFL has a larger audience advantage among women

Each horizontal connection compares AFL and NRL audience reach for one gender in one year (2023 or 2024). The distance between dots represents AFL's audience advantage. Figures are annual audience estimates, not average viewers per match.

NRLAFL

Key finding: In 2024, AFL reached about 1.02 million more women than NRL, compared with about 0.76 million more men. Both sports' estimates increased from 2023 to 2024.

Source: Roy Morgan Single Source, AFL vs NRL television viewers by gender, 2023–2024 (18 March 2025; chart on page 1). Base: Australians aged 14+. 03 / VIEWING HABITS

The changing television landscape.

A second lens on sport broadcasting: how Australians use paid streaming, catch-up TV and short-form video generally. These measures are not sports-only.

Important distinction: The ACMA charts below describe all video viewing by Australian adults, not sport-specific television audiences. Different media categories can overlap: one person can use several services.

Figure 6 / Streaming, broadcast and short-form video · Advanced slope chart

Broadcast TV recovered in 2025; paid streaming held steady

Compare all four media types in the ACMA 2024 and 2025 adult surveys. The y-axis shows the share of Australians who used each type of video service during the previous seven days, not time spent watching.

Key finding: Traditional free-to-air television rose from 46% to 52% (+6 percentage points), while paid streaming remained at 68% and short-form video fell from 59% to 52% (−7 points).

Source: ACMA, How we watch and listen to content (March 2026), executive summary. Base: Australian adults, past seven days. Media types overlap; percentage-point changes are not audience counts.

Figure 7 / Four-panel comparative unit chart · Advanced small multiples

Four different ways Australians watched video in 2025

Four aligned 100-cell grids share the same scale. Each filled square represents one percentage point of Australian adults who used that video type in the previous seven days. Categories overlap; do not add them together.

Key finding: Paid streaming reached 68% of adults, compared with 52% for broadcast TV and short-form video and 44% for catch-up TV. These are separate measures, not shares of one total.

Source: ACMA media consumption survey, 2025 results (published March 2026). All video viewing, not sports-specific; 100 squares are a visual encoding of the published percentage, not raw respondent records.

04 / ATTENDANCE

What changes when fans go to games?

Individual grounds become geographic clusters, then match-day totals. Follow the path from where crowds gather to why annual figures change.

Figure 8 / Crowd concentration · Advanced hexagonal bin map

Where do match-day crowds form geographic clusters?

The national symbol map showed individual grounds. This different map idiom combines nearby venues into nine equal-area 175 km-radius hexagons. Colour and short labels show total 2025 entries per geographic bin. Hover or select a hexagon to investigate the group.

Insight: A Melbourne-area cell groups three nearby stadiums with 4.76 million recorded entries; the Perth cell combines 1.07 million. Clusters reveal where the calendar concentrates match-day visits, without suggesting the attendees live in those places.

Source: AFL Tables, 2025 attendances by venue. Calculated from the same 18 stadium records as Figure 5; all bins together total 8,257,027 crowd entries. Equal-area binning in Australian Albers projection; converted back to geographic coordinates for display. Bin boundaries are analytical, not administrative.

Counting rule: Each attendance is one entry to a match. A person attending twice is counted twice. We use AFL Tables for all the venue and season figures so totals are internally consistent.

Figure 9 / Match-day intensity · Advanced season heatmap

Finals typically draw larger crowds per match

Each cell shows average crowd per game, not season total. Comparing per-match averages makes the 9–11 finals games more comparable with the much larger home-and-away schedule. The 2020–21 pandemic affected attendance.

Key finding: In 2026, finals averaged approximately 68,751 spectators per game versus 37,551 for home-and-away games. COVID-related disruption in 2020–21 is visible in both phases.

Source: AFL Tables, attendances by season (2015–2026). Figures are recorded match entries divided by the number of games with attendance data; finals and home-and-away values are separate.

Figure 10 / Explaining a year-on-year change · Advanced waterfall

Finals softened—but did not erase—the 2025 crowd decline

Follow the two-step bridge from 2024 to 2025. Orange shows the home-and-away shortfall; teal shows additional finals entries; the final dark bar is the net difference. The dashed connectors preserve the running change.

Insight: The 67,417-entry finals increase offset 69% of the 97,161-entry home-and-away decline. The season still finished 29,744 attendances below 2024.

Source: AFL Tables, season totals for 2024 and 2025. All figures are event entries rather than unique people. The net difference uses identical scope and source for both seasons.

Figure 11 / A complete attendance whole · Advanced treemap

Four grounds account for most AFL crowd entries

Every one of the 18 AFL venues is included: the tile areas sum to 100% of 2025 entries. Large venues carry names and percentages; smaller tiles retain a hover tooltip. This is a complete whole, not a selective top-venues chart.

Insight: The four busiest venues—the MCG, Marvel Stadium, Adelaide Oval and Optus Stadium—account for 81.7% of AFL match entries, leaving 18.3% spread across 14 other grounds. This concentration sets up the inequality curve in Figure 14.

Source: AFL Tables, 2025 attendance by venue. Full 18-venue total = 8,257,027 (including finals). Hover for exact values; very small tiles are intentionally too small to label.

Figure 12 / The role of match volume · Advanced rank-shift diagram

More fixtures can change the stadium leaderboard

Trace each of the eight most-attended venues from total crowd entries (left) to its rank for average crowd per game (right). Rankings are calculated across all 18 venues, and only the same eight venues are compared here. Marvel and Optus are highlighted because their order changes most.

Key finding: Marvel Stadium drops from 2nd in total entries to 5th by average attendance per game. Optus Stadium rises from 4th to 2nd. A stadium hosting many matches can rank highly for total crowds without having the highest average crowd.

Source: AFL Tables, 2025 crowds by venue. Average crowd = total entries divided by games hosted. Lines show ranking changes rather than trends over time.

Figure 13 / State-to-match-phase flows · Advanced Sankey diagram

Match-day visits are concentrated in Victoria—and the regular season

Hover over a flow to trace match attendances from the state hosting a game to its competition phase. Ribbon thickness represents crowd entries, not stadium capacity or unique individuals.

Key finding: Victoria accounts for 57.7% of recorded 2025 crowd entries. Most entries were from home-and-away matches (92.7% nationally); finals made up the remainder.

Source: AFL Tables, 2025 attendances by venue, including home-and-away and finals. Every one of the 8,257,027 entries is allocated exactly once. State refers to stadium location, not supporter residence.

Figure 14 / Crowd inequality · Advanced Lorenz concentration curve

How unequal are AFL stadium crowds, really?

The teal curve accumulates stadium crowds from the smallest to the largest of 18 venues. The dashed diagonal is the equal-distribution reference. The farther the curve bends below it, the more crowded the leading venues become.

Key finding: The three busiest grounds alone captured 68.8% of recorded 2025 crowd entries. The venue-concentration Gini coefficient is 0.72; the zero-to-one measure is descriptive, not a ranking of stadium quality.

Source: AFL Tables, 2025 attendance by venue, all 18 venues included. Each venue appears once. Curve and Gini calculated from exact season totals, including finals.

What the story shows: Reach measures potential viewers; geography shapes both live broadcast access and the location of fixtures; streaming and free TV attract overlapping populations; stadium entries depend on crowd size and how many games are scheduled. The resulting concentration and year-on-year changes are more informative than simply naming the largest venue.
